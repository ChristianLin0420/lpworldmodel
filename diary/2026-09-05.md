# 2026-09-05 — Round 7: the representation, not the objective

## 0. What changed

Six rounds and roughly 120 paired contrasts have now been run. Every one of them changed the
**training objective**, the **planner**, or the **predictor** on top of a representation that was
never varied: `feature=cls`, a **single 384-dimensional token for the whole image**.

They produced exactly one positive, and it is not a world-model result — plan-time consensus
beats a compute-matched single model by **+0.232 [+0.130, +0.334]** (`2026-09-03` §16.9), which
says something about ensembling and nothing about the objective, the latent or the rollout.

Round 7 changes the representation and nothing else.

The reason is a measurement that needed no dynamics, no contrast and no new GPU-hours, because
the probe had already been run and its answer was sitting in `assets/latent_probe_columns*.json`
unread.

**§8 re-ranks the whole campaign by success rate on matched seeds. Six corrections to this diary's own numbers are in §7**, one of which (§7.3) undercuts the
motivation stated immediately below: the orientation probe's headline reading is wrong, and the
probe fails the campaign's own screen.

**Two further corrections**, both found by recomputing every quoted
figure from the archive while rebuilding the figures: `patchdecode` needs **n = 20**, not the n = 36
I cancelled it over, and the `agent_pos_diff` divergence detector is **94.5 % accurate**, not the
clean separator the eight tabulated arms suggested. The second one changes how the detector may be
used — flag, not gate.

## 1. The measurement that redirects the campaign

![the orientation probe](assets/2026-09-05/orientation-probe.png)

Ridge-decode the block's pose from a **single frozen frame**. No predictor, no rollout, no
planner — just: is the information *there*? The number reported is median absolute angular error
in degrees, and the reference that matters is **not 90°**. PushT's T-shaped block settles into a
canonical orientation, so the angle is strongly non-uniform and the best achievable **constant**
prediction already scores **14.51°**. That is the bound a representation has to beat to be
carrying any orientation information at all.

| arm | representation | n | median angular error | vs the 14.51° bound |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`PiWM-columns`** | **patch tokens** | 13 | **9.58°** | −4.9°, **7th of 9 arms below it — see §7.3** |
| `LpWM-ltv-d2048` | cls, 5× width | 6 | 14.64° | +0.1°, *at* the bound |
| **`LpWM-ltv`** *(the baseline)* | cls | 16 | **15.72°** | **+1.2°, worse than the bound** |
| `PiWM-drop95` | patch, 95 % dropped | 7 | 16.01° | +1.5°, worse than the bound |

**The baseline's latent carries no usable orientation information.** At 15.72° you would do
better predicting the block's canonical angle every single time. PushT is a task about *aligning
a T-shaped block*. The representation that every one of ~120 contrasts was built on cannot see
the variable the task is about.

**Width is not the constraint.** `d2048` is five times wider and lands exactly on the bound.

> ⚠ **The probe is capacity-confounded — see §2c.** `PiWM-columns` carries **98 304** latent
> dimensions against `LpWM-ltv`'s **384**, a 256× gap, because a patch arm carries
> `num_patches × proj_dim`. A larger representation decoding the angle better is not evidence
> that *tokens* are what carry it. The claim below is what the probe appeared to show; §2c's
> dimension-matched grid is what will actually test it, and until those 40 runs land this
> section states a hypothesis, not a result.

**Tokens are.** `PiWM-columns` is a "single-factor" change *in the config* — its arm spec is byte-identical
to the baseline (`ARMS[PiWM-columns]="ltv 1.0 5e-4"`), with only `encoder.feature: cls → patch`
differing — and it is the one arm in the archive that beats the bound.

**And the dose-response was meant to say it is the tokens, not the word "patch"** — `PiWM-drop95`
is also patch-feature but discards 95 % of its tokens and falls back to 16.01°. That argument is
now doubly weak: `drop95` keeps the same 98 304-dim latent (dropping is stochastic, at training
time only), so it does **not** vary capacity either, and 4 of its 7 seeds are in total
representational collapse.

### Why this explains the shape of the whole record

If the representation is missing the task's controlled variable, then:

- **No loss defined on that latent can recover it.** Improving prediction of an orientation-blind
  code is exactly the null result six rounds kept producing.
- **Latent accuracy should not predict planning**, and it does not: across a **33× range** of
  5-step rollout error, planning does not follow, and it is not even monotone (`2026-09-04` §7.3).
- **Screened targets should fail under intervention**, and all three did — `S_model` at −0.260
  after passing the screen, and K=5 rollout error at −0.200 with the intervention already sitting
  in the archive (§7.2, §7.3).
- **Ensembling should help a little and cap out**, and it does: members' errors are independent
  and average at the 1/√M rate, but the committee only ties a genuinely strong member
  (`2026-09-03` §16.9).

This is the first hypothesis in the campaign that accounts for the *pattern* of results rather
than one result.

## 2. What the CEM evidence does and does not say

I want to be precise here, because §7.3 is a fresh lesson about believing observational patterns.

| arm | feature | CEM mean | vs baseline |
|---|---|---|---|
| `PiWM-patchdecode` | patch | **0.520** | highest non-consensus arm mean in the campaign |
| `PiWM-columns` | patch | 0.418 | **+0.072 [−0.064, +0.207]**, n=12 — a *null* |
| `LpWM-ltv` | cls | 0.357 | — |
| `PiWM-drop95` | patch, 95 % dropped | 0.006 | destroyed |

**The single-factor contrast is a positive-leaning null**, not a positive. It is the largest n of
any treated arm in the campaign and its interval still spans zero. That is the honest statement,
and round 7's S2 exists to settle it.

**A test I ran and am deliberately not reporting as evidence.** Ranking patch arms against cls
arms campaign-wide gives p = 0.0065 — and it is worthless, because it compares three patch arms
against forty-six cls arms most of which carry actively harmful objectives. It measures those
objectives, not the feature. Recorded here so nobody re-derives it and believes it.

**The strong claim is the probe**, which is a direct measurement of a frozen representation and
needs no contrast at all.

### The counterexample I have to put on the record

`LpWM-ltv-d2048` sits **at** the orientation bound (14.64°, i.e. orientation-blind) and has the
**highest raw CEM mean in the table, 0.587**. Taken at face value that is a counterexample to
round 7's premise: the best planner cannot see the block's angle.

It does not survive pairing, and the reason is the confound this campaign already fixed once.

| | seeds evaluated | mean |
|---|---|---|
| `LpWM-ltv-d2048` | **0, 1, 2**, 3, 4, 5 | 0.587 |
| `LpWM-ltv` (baseline) | 3 … 15 | 0.357 |

Its three highest scores (0.72, 0.78, 0.68) are on seeds **0–2, which the baseline has no
fixed-instrument evaluation for at all** — so the 0.587 is an *unpaired* mean over a different
seed population. On the three seeds they actually share, the paired contrast is
**+0.020 [−1.464, +1.504]**: an interval three units wide, carrying no information whatsoever
(one of the three d2048 seeds scores 0.00).

So d2048 is **not** a demonstrated counterexample — but it is **not excluded either**, and it is
the single largest threat to the premise. If width really does buy planning without buying
orientation, the representation story is wrong and the campaign should be widening rather than
tokenising. That is cheap to settle — d2048 needs seeds 6–12 to become pairable — and it should
be settled before round 7's result is believed either way.

*(This is also why panel B of the figure above is labelled an association and not a lever:
four arms, ρ = −0.80 at p = 0.20, and **not monotone** precisely because of this row.)*

Three numbers govern everything below:

|---|---|
| MDE of a **retrained** arm at n = 8, 80 % power | **0.150** |

**Detecting +0.072 at 80 % power needs n = 69 seeds.** S2 as launched (n = 12 → 15) cannot

*(Corrected from n = 36 — see §7.1.)*
Adding three or four seeds to a contrast that needs forty more is theatre.

Two escape routes were tested and one is closed:

* **Episode-level pairing does not help.** Cluster-robust SE 0.0589 against seed-level 0.0615 —

* **Committees halve the sd** — 0.071 for committee arms against 0.141 for healthy single arms,
  and 0.071 is exactly the 50-episode binomial floor. Consistent with the measured independence
  of member errors (ρ̄ ≈ 0.001), where averaging M members cuts the model-variance by ~M.

## 2c. REDESIGN II — the comparison was never dimension-matched

Two faults in round 7's own design, both real, both now fixed.

### The patch/cls comparison confounded the feature with a 256× capacity change

A patch arm carries `num_patches × proj_dim` latent values; a cls arm carries `proj_dim`. At
this campaign's defaults:

| arm | feature | proj_dim | tokens | **total latent dims** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `LpWM-ltv` | cls | 384 | 1 | **384** |
| `LpWM-ltv-d2048` | cls | 2048 | 1 | 2048 |
| `PiWM-columns` | patch | 384 | 256 | **98 304** |

**`PiWM-columns` vs `LpWM-ltv` is a 256× capacity change wearing the label of a feature change.**
So is the orientation probe: of *course* a 98 304-dimensional representation decodes the block's
angle better than a 384-dimensional one. `LpWM-ltv-d2048` — the only width control the campaign
ever ran — is 5.3×, nowhere near enough to control it.

![the aligned grid](assets/2026-09-05/arch-aligned-grid.svg)

The fix is a grid matched on **total latent dimension**. `num_patches = (img_size/patch_size)²`
and `img_size` is 224, so `patch_size` is the token knob (14 → 256, 56 → 16, 112 → 4, 224 → 1),
and `encoder.proj_dim` is settable independently of the ViT width:

| total dims | cls | patch |
|---|---|---|
| **384** | `LpWM-ltv` *(exists)* | **`LpWM-ltv-p1`** — `patch_size 224`, 1 token |
| **1536** | **`LpWM-ltv-d1536`** | **`PiWM-cols-p4`** — `patch_size 112`, 4 tokens |
| **6144** | **`LpWM-ltv-d6144`** | **`PiWM-cols-p16`** — `patch_size 56`, 16 tokens |
| 98 304 | infeasible | `PiWM-columns` *(exists)* |

Each **row** is a cls-vs-patch contrast at fixed capacity; each **column** is a capacity ladder
at fixed feature. Nothing in rounds 1–7 could separate those two. **40 runs launched.**

*(The arm names carry `PROJ_D` deliberately: reusing `LpWM-ltv` at a different `proj_dim` would
file it under the baseline's key in `collect_evals` and silently pool two architectures.)*

### Consensus had two hyperparameters and needs none

![leave-one-out consensus](assets/2026-09-05/arch-loo-consensus.svg)

Rounds 5–6 swept `vote{2,3,5,8,12} × {borda, median, cvar1, cvar2, max}`. The audit (§16.9)
showed the member set was a **step function of the episode block**, so the ten "seeds" of
`vote5-median` were really **two committees**, and the five rules correlate **r = 0.87–0.98**.
Two knobs, one measurement.

`scripts/plan_vote_loo.sh` removes both. For episode block *b* the committee is every finished
seed **except** *b*, so **M = n − 1 is set by the data**, and the rule is fixed to
median-of-ranks — scale-free, defined at every M, and the best-powered rule in the sweep.

**M must also be matched across the arms compared** — an unmatched M would confound committee
size with the feature, the same error as the unmatched latent dimension.

*In practice M is capped by the machine, not by the data — and by **two** separate walls.*

**Wall 1, memory, at M = 11.** `planning/ensemble.py` stacks members along the **patch axis**, so a
patch member costs `num_patches` slots where a cls member costs 1. At M = 11 that is
**11 × 256 = 2816** stacked slots against 11, and every M = 11 patch committee died with a genuine
`torch.cuda.OutOfMemoryError` (`F.gelu`, allocating 100 MiB) while every cls committee ran. This is
what fixes M = 5: it is the largest patch committee that fits in memory.

**Wall 2, walltime, at M = 5.** Fitting is not the same as finishing. Measured single-model eval
cost is **cls 40 min, patch 64 min**, and a committee costs `base + (M−1) × incr` with the increment
scaling like the base (cls 32.5 min → patch ≈ 52). That predicts **cls M=5 = 172 min** (measured
169 ✓) and **patch M=5 = 272 min** — against the **235 min (03:55) cap** of the 4 h GPU partitions.
All 10 patch committees duly **timed out at 03:55:23 having produced nothing.**

`plan_mcurve.sh` already routes M ≥ 6 to a 9:55 backfill script, but *"M ≥ 6" is a cls rule* and
silently mis-routes patch. `plan_vote_loo.sh` now routes on the **predicted cost** instead, which
gets both features right, and the patch committees are rerunning on the long window.

So **both sides run at M = 5** — the cls side capped *down* from its feasible 11 to match, because
keeping the larger feasible M on one side only is exactly the confound this design removes.
**16 cls and 12 patch committees launched at M = 5.**

*(The asymmetry is real and not merely a resource limit: stacking on the patch axis makes a patch
committee 256× more expensive in that dimension than a cls one, which is what produces both walls.
Any future committee work on patch representations needs either a member axis separate from the
patch axis, or chunked scoring.)*

**Correction.** An earlier version of this section attributed the whole problem to memory and called
M = 5 "the largest that fits", which read as though M = 5 patch was running. It was not — it fit and
then ran out of clock. The M = 5 choice is unchanged (wall 1 is real and binds at M = 11), but the
reason M = 5 had produced no data was wall 2, and a missing sbatch route rather than a hard limit.
Worth recording because a TIMEOUT and an OOM look identical downstream: both leave `n = 0` with
checkpoints on disk, which is the same "absent reads as no-data" failure `resolve_arm` was written
for.

## 2d. The two detectors the campaign never had

![the instrument](assets/2026-09-05/instrument.png)

`analysis/run_health.py`. Six rounds ran on one gate, `err/rel_mse ≥ 0.5`, which misses both
failure modes actually encountered:

* **`sparsity/effective_dim == 0`** — total representational collapse. A constant-output model
  has `rel_mse ≈ 0`, so the gate scores it *healthy*. `PiWM-drop95` and `PiWM-incr-eps0p001`
  have their **highest-scoring** seeds in this state, which inverts their ranking.
* **`median agent_pos_diff > 100 px`** — a diverged rollout. Nothing in the training logs sees
  it: the model can be a fine 1-step predictor and still drive the simulation off the table.
  On the eight arms tabulated in the 2026-09-04 §7 box, healthy sits at **13–28 px** and failing
  at **1000–2700 px**. **That is not archive-wide** — over all 49 arms with traces the ranges
  overlap, and the threshold is a good classifier rather than a clean separator: **273/289 runs
  correct, with 14 healthy runs above 100 px** (§7.2). Use it to flag, not to gate.

**Of the runs these two flag, 139 would not be caught by `rel_mse ≥ 0.5`.** Both are free — one
reads a wandb summary, the other reads traces already on disk.

### S1 (superseded) · the token dose-response

![S1](assets/2026-09-05/arch-s1-tokendrop.svg)

`TOKEN_DROP ∈ {0.25, 0.50, 0.75, 0.90}`, eight seeds each. The endpoints already exist
(`0.0` = `PiWM-columns`, `0.95` = `PiWM-drop95`), so this buys the interior of a six-point curve.

**This is the cleanest causal design available here, because it never compares patch against
cls.** A patch-vs-cls contrast confounds the feature with everything else that differs; a ladder
*inside* the patch family cannot. If angular error and CEM success degrade together as tokens are
removed, then tokens → orientation → planning is causal rather than correlational.

*Falsifier:* CEM flat across the ladder while angular error climbs. That would say orientation
decodability is yet another screened quantity that does not survive intervention — the fourth —
and would close the representation direction as firmly as §7.2 closed R6.

### S2 · power the single-factor contrast — ~~3 runs~~ **CANCELLED (see §2b)**

> Detecting +0.072 at 80 % power needs **n = 69**. Three seeds do not approach that, and the
> contrast is capacity-confounded regardless (§2c). Superseded by the aligned grid.

`PiWM-columns` at seeds 0–2. The contrast is +0.072 [−0.064, +0.207] at n=12; `columns` holds
seeds 3–15 and the baseline holds 0–15, so these are the cheapest three points that widen the

*A trap worth recording:* wave12's `PiWM-columns` has **no `ARM_FEAT` entry** and defaults to

### S3 · patch consensus — 10 evals, no training

![S3](assets/2026-09-05/arch-s3-patchvote.svg)

An M=5 committee whose members are `PiWM-columns` checkpoints, same block→family rule and same
rule (`median`) as the cls committees, so it is committee-against-committee on the same episode
blocks.

This composes the campaign's only two leads, and it is a **directional prediction with a
falsifier**. §16.9 measured that what caps consensus is the quality of the *best member*, not
correlation between members. If that is right, a committee whose members can see the controlled
variable should sit above one that cannot.

*Falsifier:* the patch committee lands at the same ~0.60 as the cls committee. Then the ceiling
is not member quality and §16.9's mechanism story is wrong.

### S5 · the width × learning-rate 2×2, finally completed — 16 runs

The counterexample above turns on one question: **does width buy planning without buying
orientation?** `scripts/run_campaign.sh:190-211` already knew the answer was unmeasured, and
said why — muP sets `used_lr = base_lr × base_width / fan_in` with `base_width` pinned at 384,
so every predictor matrix at D = 2048 gets

    D=384  ->  5e-4 × 384/384  = 5.000e-4
    D=2048 ->  5e-4 × 384/2048 = 9.375e-5      (5.3× lower)

**So "width helps" is confounded with a 5.3× learning-rate reduction**, and a too-high LR would
also explain the baseline's catastrophic-zero seeds (`LpWM-ltv` spans 0.00–0.66 over 13 seeds).

`wave6` and `wave7` were written to complete that 2×2 — D = 384 at d2048's rate, and D = 2048 at
the baseline's rate — with the comment *"Zero code change: mup_lr is the 3rd ARMS field."*
**Neither was ever trained.** Both cells have zero run directories.

| | lr 5.0e-4 | lr 9.4e-5 |
|---|---|---|
| **D = 384** | 0.357 (n=13) — the baseline | **never run** → `wave6` |
| **D = 2048** | **never run** → `wave7` | 0.587 (n=6) — d2048 |

Only the diagonal exists, which is exactly the configuration that cannot separate the two
factors. Sixteen runs complete it.

*Falsifier for the premise:* if `LpWM-ltv-lr9e5` (D = 384 at the low rate) reaches d2048's ~0.59,
then the "width helps" result was always a learning-rate result, d2048 stops being a

## 7. Two numbers in this diary do not survive recomputation

Both were found while rebuilding the round-7 figures, by recomputing every quoted number from the
archive instead of copying it forward. Both are errors in my own text, and both are recorded here
rather than silently edited, because the point of §2b is that a number nobody re-derives is a number
nobody has checked.

### 7.1 `patchdecode` needs n = 20, not n = 36

The required-n figures in §2b come from the two-sided normal approximation at 80 % power,
`n = ((z₀.₉₇₅ + z₀.₈₀) · sd / δ)²`, applied to each contrast's **own** paired sd.

| contrast | δ | paired sd | n | recomputed n_req | diary said |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `columns` vs baseline | +0.0717 | 0.2131 | 12 | **69.4** | 69 ✓ |
| `patchdecode` vs `-detach` | +0.1400 | 0.2235 | 8 | **20.0** | 36 ✗ |

`columns` reproduces exactly, so the formula and the pipeline are right; only the `patchdecode`
figure is wrong. It is not a mis-attributed contrast either — the two other pairings give 14.6
(`patchdecode` vs `LpWM-ltv`) and 3938 (`-detach` vs `LpWM-ltv`), and reproducing 36 at δ = +0.140
would need a paired sd of 0.2998, which no pairing has.

**This runs in `patchdecode`'s favour, and it reverses the decision.** S4 was cancelled for being
under-powered at n → 12; the real requirement is 20, not 36, so the shortfall is 8 seeds rather than
24. I cancelled it citing a price two-thirds higher than the true one.

**S4 is therefore restored and launched at n = 20** (§3, S4): seeds 11–22 for both `patchdecode` and
its `-detach` control, 24 runs, ≈ 175 GPU-h. This is the whole point of recomputing quoted numbers —
the error did not just mis-state a figure, it killed an experiment.

### 7.2 The px separation is an eight-arm claim, not an archive-wide one

The 2026-09-04 §7 retraction box says `median agent_pos_diff` gives *"13–28 px for every healthy arm
against 1000–2700 px for every failing one, with no overlap."* That is true of the **eight arms it
tabulates**. It is not true of the archive:

| | n | px range |
|---|---|---|
| per run, SR > 0.1 | 142 | 5.8 – 1204.5 |
| per run, SR ≤ 0.1 | 147 | 27.9 – 6902.7 |
| per arm, healthy | 27 | 7.5 – 1138.3 |
| per arm, failing | 22 | 122.0 – 2966.7 |

The ranges overlap at both levels. The worst healthy arm is `PiWM-support-w0p1` at **1138 px** and
the best failing arm is `PiWM-actinfo` at **122 px**.

**The detector is still good — it is just not perfect.** At the 100 px threshold it gets
**273 of 289 runs right (94.5 %)**: 145/147 failing runs caught, 128/142 healthy runs cleared, with
**14 healthy runs above 100 px** and 2 failing runs below it.

That distinction decides how it may be used. "No overlap" would license a **hard gate**; 94.5 %
does not — gating on it would discard 14 healthy runs, about a tenth of the healthy population. So
`agent_pos_diff > 100 px` stays a **flag**, reported beside SR, and never a kill condition. §2d and the P1b′ entry in §1 are amended accordingly.

This is the same failure as the `d_action` units error and the `h8/h1` collapse detector: a
diagnostic that looked clean on the handful of arms I inspected, promoted to a rule before it was
measured on the rest.

This is what the leave-one-out design in §2c was *meant* to fix — one committee per block, so k = n
and the two levels coincide. **As implemented it did not**; see §7.7.

### 7.5 The 384-dim row of my own grid does not measure what it claims

The aligned grid varies `patch_size` to set the token count at fixed `proj_dim`, so that each row is
a cls-vs-patch contrast at equal total latent. The arithmetic is right. The **encoder** is not.

`models/vit_encoder.py`:

```python
self.patch_embed = nn.Conv2d(3, dim, kernel_size=patch_size, stride=patch_size)
```

`patch_size` sets the token count **and** the patch embedding's receptive field. At `patch_size=224`
that is a single 224×224 convolution — one linear map of the entire 150,528-pixel image to 384 dims
— with `num_patches = 1`, so there is no inter-token attention to do. `LpWM-ltv-p1` is therefore not
"cls at matched dimension". It is a structurally different, degenerate encoder.

**And it behaves like one. All four evaluated seeds are flagged:**

| run | eff_dim | rel_mse | agent px | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ltv-p1_s3` | 12.9 | 0.0278 | **1491.0** | DIVERGED |
| `ltv-p1_s5` | 15.2 | 0.0250 | **1413.8** | DIVERGED |
| `ltv-p1_s6` | **0.0** | 0.0000 | 186.6 | **COLLAPSED**, DIVERGED |
| `ltv-p1_s9` | 15.6 | 0.0276 | **1666.8** | DIVERGED |

Per-seed success 0.00, 0.00, 0.02, 0.04 against the baseline's 0.357. Healthy arms sit at 13–28 px;
these are two orders of magnitude out. **`rel_mse` is 0.025–0.028 for all of them — inside the
healthy range — so the pre-registered `rel_mse >= 0.5` gate scores every one of them healthy.** The
divergence detector earns its place here, four rounds after it should have existed.

**So the −0.365 [−0.684, −0.046] the autopilot printed at n=4 is not a feature effect.** It is a
healthy baseline against an arm whose rollouts leave the table. Reading it as "patch tokens are
worse at 384 dims" would repeat exactly the error §7.3 just corrected — attributing to the feature
something the measurement does not support.

**What survives.** The confound this grid was built to remove (dimension) *is* removed. But
`patch_size` cannot vary the token count without also varying the patch receptive field, so the
grid trades one confound for a weaker one rather than eliminating it. That is tolerable at
`patch_size` 112 and 56 (4 and 16 tokens, coarse but structurally normal) and **not** tolerable at
224, where the encoder degenerates.

**Consequence:** the **384 row is uninterpretable as a feature contrast** and will be reported as
such — it cannot distinguish "patch tokens are worse" from "a 224×224 convolution is a bad image
encoder", and the second is obviously true. The 1,536 and 6,144 rows stand. The `ltv-p1` seeds are
left running to n=8, because the honest report of that row is a *measured* divergence rather than a
dropped arm, but no feature claim will rest on it.

I designed this row and called the grid "verified exact" on the arithmetic. The arithmetic was
exact and the comparison was still not clean.

### 7.6 The row comparison may be unfixable; the column comparison is not

§7.5 blames `patch_size=224`. It is worth asking whether a *better* 384-dim patch arm exists, and
the answer is that the row comparison is confounded by construction.

Total latent = **tokens × width**. To put a patch arm at 384 total dims you must pick one of:

| option | tokens | width | what it changes vs `LpWM-ltv` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `patch_size 224` (what I ran) | 1 | 384 | a 224×224 conv, no inter-token attention — **degenerate** |
| `patch_size 112`, `proj_dim 96` | 4 | 96 | structurally fine, but **per-token width drops 4×** |
| `patch_size 56`, `proj_dim 24` | 16 | 24 | fine, but per-token width drops **16×** |

There is no setting that holds total dimension fixed, keeps the encoder non-degenerate, **and**
leaves per-token width alone — because the two factors multiply to the quantity being held fixed.
A single-point cls-vs-patch contrast at matched capacity is therefore not cleanly achievable. Adding
`cols-p4-d96` would not repair the row; it would swap the receptive-field confound for a width one.

**But the grid is two-dimensional, and the other axis is clean.** Each feature has its own capacity
ladder over the *same* total dimensions:

* **cls**, capacity added as **width**: 384 → 1,536 → 6,144
* **patch**, capacity added as **tokens** at fixed width 384: 1 → 4 → 16 tokens

Comparing the **slopes** of those two ladders asks a well-posed question — *does capacity bought as
tokens buy more planning than the same capacity bought as width?* — and it needs no cross-feature
match at any single point, so none of the confounds above apply to it. Over 1,536 → 6,144 both
features are structurally normal (`patch_size` 112 and 56), so that segment is interpretable even
though the 384 end is not.

**This is the comparison round 7 should be read on.** The rows were the stated design and one of the
three is broken; the columns were built as a secondary control and are the part that survives
contact with the encoder. No new runs are needed — the arms already queued deliver it.

### 7.7 The leave-one-out design did not do leave-one-out

The point of `plan_vote_loo.sh` was to remove the member-count knob *and* give every episode block
its own committee, so that k = n and the pseudo-replication of §7.4 disappears. Membership read back
from the eval logs of `PiWM-loo5-ltv`:

```
block s0  → {1,2,3,4,5}      block s4  → {0,1,2,3,5}
block s1  → {0,2,3,4,5}      block s5  → {0,1,2,3,4}
block s2  → {0,1,3,4,5}      block s6..s15 → {0,1,2,3,4}   ← all ten identical
block s3  → {0,1,2,4,5}
```

**Six distinct committees over sixteen blocks, eleven of them sharing one.** Worse than
`vote3-median`, which the same section had just shown to be the better-supported arm with four
*balanced* committees over twelve blocks.

**The bug is mine and it is the interaction of two decisions that were each defensible.** The
selection rule was *"the first M seeds in order, excluding b"*, which is genuine leave-one-out only
while `b` falls inside the first `M+1` seeds. That held when M = n−1. It stopped holding the moment
M was capped to 5 by the GPU-memory wall (§7.5 of the same design note): for every block b ≥ 6 the
exclusion is vacuous, because b was never among the first five to begin with.

So the memory cap did not merely shrink the committees — it **silently reintroduced the exact
pseudo-replication the script existed to remove**, and it did so in the arm I had just called "the
one with k = n".

**Fixed** by taking the M members from a *rotating* start indexed by the block rather than from the
front of the list: still deterministic, still excludes b, still honours the cap, and now yields
**16 distinct committees from 16 blocks**. The old runs are kept under the `loo5` tag and the
corrected ones launched as `loor5`, so the two are distinguishable in the archive rather than
silently merged.

**What this does to the numbers.** `PiWM-loo5-ltv`'s block-level +0.189 [+0.077, +0.302] at n = 13
is not wrong as a measurement, but it is 13 blocks over 6 committees, not 13 independent draws, and
at the committee level it reads +0.311 [−0.158, +0.780] on k = 3 of those 6 — an interval about
four times wider. **`vote3-median` therefore remains the best-supported consensus arm in the
archive**, and the claim I made earlier today that `loo5` displaced it is withdrawn.

## 8. Ranked by success rate, and what survives the variance

Re-gathered on the user's criterion: **mean success rate against the baseline at the same total
latent dimension**, no confidence intervals used to declare anything null. 112 arms, 2026-09-01 to
2026-09-05.

### 8.1 The seed set has to match, or the ranking is an artefact

Comparing an arm's mean to the baseline's *registered* mean compares different seed sets. The
baseline holds seeds 3–15; almost every treated arm holds 3–10. On those eight shared seeds
`LpWM-ltv` averages **0.3925**, against its registered **0.3569** — so **every 8-seed arm receives a

note); it moved 35 apparent winners down to 16.

`PiWM-solo{m}` is baseline checkpoint *m* replayed on episode block *b*. The 50-cell matrix
decomposes as:

| source | share of variance | sd |
|---|---|---|
| **checkpoint (training seed)** | **81.5 %** | 0.128 |
| episode block | **16.0 %** | 0.030 |
| residual | 13 % | — |

A single unchanged model spans only **0.04–0.24 across blocks**; the 0.165–0.585 span is across
**ten different checkpoints**. *(An earlier note in this campaign attributed that span to blocks.
It is training-seed variance with block variance already averaged out.)*

**Of the eleven training-side arms tabulated, only the top two clear the bar; five are negative once seeds are matched.** Only `relu-p2` and
`patchdecode` clear the bar on the training side.

### 8.4 "Add a reconstruction signal" is not the recurring mechanism

I reported the decoder family as the one repeating mechanism among winners. It is not — the pixel
gradient's effect **flips sign with the representation**:

| | gradient on vs off | detached decoder vs none |
|---|---|---|
| cls, 384 | `decode` − `-detach` = **−0.060** | `decode-detach` − `ltv` = **+0.055** |
| patch, 98 304 | `patchdecode` − `-detach` = **+0.140** | `patchdecode-detach` − `columns` = **−0.055** |

At 384 dims the whole "decoder win" is +0.055 from the arm whose gradient is **provably
disconnected** (`z_dec = z_emb.detach()`, `models/visual_world_model.py:1630`), and the real
gradient costs 0.06 on top. And `decode-detach` is measurably an *unchanged re-draw*: attaching a
frozen decoder perturbs the world model about as much as changing the seed does (same-seed median
|rel. difference| in `effective_dim` 0.081 against the baseline's own seed-to-seed 0.065; `mse_t1`
0.176 against 0.332), with a same-seed success correlation of **+0.061**.

### 8.5 One axis survives arm-demeaning, and it is not a representation axis

Across arms almost every diagnostic correlates with success, because 31 of 56 trained arms are
broken. Demeaned **within** arm, over 174 runs in the 19 non-degenerate ones:

| axis | within-arm ρ |
|---|---|
| **one-step latent prediction** (`val/z_loss`, `mse_t1`, `rel_mse`) | **−0.680** |
| compounding growth `h8/h1` | **−0.001** |
| probe decodability, among arms that encode anything | **+0.45** pos / **+0.51** ang (inverted) |
| `l0_frac` / `dead_unit_frac` | dead at every threshold |
| **`effective_dim`** | **+0.342, p = 3.1e-11 — NOT dead** (corrected, §9.5) |

Three consequences. **Compounding does not matter** — which closes T6/R1's premise independently of
the K sweep. **Probe decodability runs backwards** among live models, confirming §7.3 from a second
direction. And `patchdecode`'s gain is specifically one-step: −20.6 % visual-latent error at h1,
−14 % on `mse_t1`, with `h8/h1` unchanged (3.87 vs 4.00).

### 8.6 The kernel, tested directly

`d_action/‖z‖` re-probed to **66 arms / 449 runs**:

| subset | n | ρ | p |
|---|---|---|---|
| all arms | 45 | +0.624 | <0.0001 |
| **healthy** (SR ≥ 0.20) | 23 | +0.316 | **0.142** |
| dead (SR < 0.20) | 22 | +0.486 | 0.022 |

The relation lives entirely in the dead arms. And the interventional test: **`PiWM-jump2` has the
highest action sensitivity of any healthy arm (0.980 against the baseline's 0.549, 1.8×) and its
success rate is baseline** (−0.035 on shared seeds). The winners barely move it at all — 0.90× to
1.07×, with `patchdecode`, the best of them, at **0.98×**.

**So the root-cause hypothesis of 2026-09-02 §8 — the action is causally inert, raise its influence
and planning improves — has now been tested directly and does not hold.** Action inertness is a
symptom of a dead model, not a lever on a live one.

### 8.7 Why no training-side axis can order this campaign

**Seven of the top eight arms are planner-side.** `vote5-borda` 0.608, `vote5-median` 0.602,
`vote5-cvar1/2` 0.585, `vote3-borda` 0.583, `vote5-max` 0.568, `loo5` 0.552 — every one loads
`LpWM-ltv` checkpoints, so their world-model diagnostics *are* the baseline's, to the last decimal.
The campaign's largest success differences were produced without changing the world model at all.

## 9. Round 7, partial: the patch side is broken and the cls ladder is the biggest effect

Still below the n = 8 floor, recorded because the health picture is already decisive.

### 9.1 Every patch arm in the grid is unhealthy

| arm | patch_size | tokens | mean | n | flagged |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `LpWM-ltv-d1536` | — (cls) | 1 | **0.550** | 6 | **0 / 9** |
| `LpWM-ltv-d6144` | — (cls) | 1 | — | 0 | **0 / 5** |
| `LpWM-ltv-p1` | 224 | 1 | 0.020 | 7 | 4 / 4 |
| `PiWM-cols-p4` | 112 | 4 | 0.155 | 4 | **4 / 5** |
| `PiWM-cols-p16` | 56 | 16 | 0.055 | 4 | **4 / 7** |

**Correction.** §7.5 reported `cols-p16` as clean and called the 6,144 row the one interpretable
comparison. That was on **two** finished runs; at seven it is **4 / 7 flagged**. Round 7 has **no**
healthy cls-vs-patch row.

### 9.2 The cause is patch granularity, not patch tokens

`PiWM-columns` — `patch_size` **14**, 256 tokens, the same `feature=patch` code path — is healthy
and scores 0.418 at n = 12. The broken arms are `patch_size` **224, 112, 56**. So the failure tracks
the *coarseness of the patch*, not the use of patch tokens.

This finishes the argument §7.5 and §7.6 started. Varying token count at fixed `proj_dim` means
varying `patch_size`, and `patch_size` is simultaneously the receptive field of
`nn.Conv2d(3, dim, kernel_size=patch_size, stride=patch_size)`. **The grid varied patch granularity
and read it as token count.** §7.5 caught the degenerate extreme at 224; the measurement now shows
the whole coarse range is affected, 56 included.

So the dimensional confound was removed and replaced by a granularity confound — and unlike the
first one, this replacement was not visible from the arithmetic. It took the divergence detector,
which the campaign did not have four rounds ago, to see it at all.

### 9.3 The clean result, and it is the largest in the campaign

The **cls capacity ladder** is fully healthy on both rungs measured:

| | dims | mean | n |
|---|---|---|---|
| `LpWM-ltv` | 384 | 0.357 | 13 |
| `LpWM-ltv-d1536` | 1 536 | **0.550** | 6 |

paired **+0.200 at n = 6** — larger than any training-objective intervention in six rounds, larger
than `patchdecode` (+0.085) and `relu-p2` (+0.068), and comparable to the consensus family.

**Not called yet:** n = 6 is below the floor, and this arm read +0.113 at n = 2, so it has moved as
seeds landed. `LpWM-ltv-d6144` has 5 healthy trained runs and no evaluations; that rung decides
whether width keeps paying or turns over.

If it holds, the campaign's answer is uncomfortable and simple: **plain width bought more than any
objective, planner or predictor change attempted**, and the representation axis that mattered was
not tokens. That would also explain §8.7 — the largest gains all came from arms that changed the
model's capacity or the planner, never its training objective.

### 9.4 Width does not turn over — training stability does

`LpWM-ltv-d6144` reads +0.048 paired where `d1536` reads +0.200, which looks like the width ladder
saturating. It is not. Splitting by the divergence detector:

| width | healthy runs | mean of the healthy | flagged |
|---|---|---|---|
| 384 | 13 / 13 | 0.357 | — |
| 1 536 | 6 / 6 | **0.550** | — |
| 6 144 | **3 / 5** | **0.660** | 2 diverged, at 756 px and 1 148 px → 0.06, 0.04 |

**Among runs that train successfully the ladder is monotonic: 0.357 → 0.550 → 0.660.** `d6144`'s
healthy seeds score 0.68, 0.60, 0.70; its two failures score 0.06 and 0.04. The collapse of the
paired effect is entirely those two seeds.

So at 6 144 the model is **better when it works and fails to train 40 % of the time**. That moves
the question from *how much width* to *why does wide training diverge*, which is a different and
more tractable target — and it is consistent with the muP evidence already in hand:
`LpWM-ltv-d2048-hilr`, which is d2048 at the **base** learning rate instead of muP's 5.3×-lower
prescription, is **5 / 5 collapsed** at `effective_dim` exactly 0. Learning-rate scaling with width
is already known to be load-bearing in this codebase.

**Two corrections to §9.1.** I reported `d6144` as "0 / 5 flagged". That was read from
`assets/run_health.json`, which was written at 04:30 before these evaluations existed; the live
detector finds 2 / 5. The cache has been regenerated (629 runs, 207 flagged, **170 of them invisible
to the pre-registered `rel_mse ≥ 0.5` gate**). And §9.3's framing — "whether width keeps paying or
turns over" — offered two answers when the data supports a third: it keeps paying, and the
constraint moves to optimisation.

### 9.5 Three corrections the design review produced, one of them urgent

An adversarial review of the design proposals recomputed my own evidence rather than building on
it, and broke three things.

**1. The +0.200 width headline is six of eight trained seeds — and the two missing ones are not
random.** `LpWM-ltv-d1536` s4 and s5 both carry `DONE=epochs=2` and have **no** `plan_outputs`
directory. Seed 5's `effective_dim` is **1.04** — effectively rank-1 — with `rel_mse` 0.061.

| if the two missing seeds land at… | effect at n = 8 |
|---|---|
| s4 healthy 0.68, **s5 collapses to 0.00** | **+0.105** |
| both at the arm mean 0.55 | +0.158 |
| both collapse | +0.020 |

against the baseline's s4 = 0.38, s5 = 0.66. So the campaign's largest clean effect may **halve**,
and it would land exactly on the M2 bar. Both evaluations are launched. **This is the same
read-before-all-seeds-land error I spent the day correcting in other people's numbers, committed in
my own headline.**

**2. `effective_dim == 0` is the wrong collapse threshold.** **48 runs sit at
`0 < effective_dim < 10` and pass all three health gates**, `d1536` s5 among them at 1.04. The gate
is now `< 10`, benchmarked against PushT's own participation ratio of **4.31** rather than against D.
`docs/measurement-protocol.md` §2 updated.

**3. §8.5's "sparsity dead at every threshold" is wrong for `effective_dim`.** Demeaned within arm
over 358 runs in 49 non-diverged arms it gives **ρ = +0.342, p = 3.1e-11** — about half the strength
of the one-step axis, but not dead. It stays un-licensed as a target for a better reason than being
dead: **four arms already reached the manipulation at fixed width and it bought nothing** —
`PiWM-lie` 27.09, `lie-sim` 27.79, `multact` 27.20, `linvar` 27.19, all ≈ +3.5 over baseline, all
planning at **0.020–0.080**. It passes screen stages 2 and 3 and fails stage 4 on evidence already
in hand.

**And one caveat on my own S1.** I told the design panel that "a 256× larger feature count buys zero
extra participation ratio". `effective_dim` is computed on
`rearrange(z, "b t p d -> (b t p) d")` — the **token axis is folded into samples**, so the statistic
is the participation ratio over the 384 per-token features and is bounded by 384 no matter how many
tokens there are. It has never measured the token axis. The comparison "25.07 cls vs 25.15 patch"
compares two quantities both capped at 384, so it is partly a property of the metric. It did not
reach the diaries, but it did shape a design brief.

### 9.6 M1 resolved: the width effect is +0.100, exactly half what I first reported

Both missing seeds landed. `LpWM-ltv-d1536` vs `LpWM-ltv`, paired on all eight shared seeds:

| seed | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `d1536` | 0.44 | 0.64 | **0.00** | 0.58 | 0.36 | 0.66 | 0.58 | 0.68 |
| baseline | 0.24 | 0.38 | **0.66** | 0.26 | 0.38 | 0.48 | 0.36 | 0.38 |

**+0.100 at n = 8**, against **+0.200 at n = 6** when I first reported it. Exactly half.

**Seed 5 is the whole difference, and it was visible on disk before it was evaluated.** Its
`effective_dim` is **1.04** — effectively rank-1 — and it scored **0.00** against the baseline's
**0.66**, the baseline's single best seed. The prediction from that alone was +0.105; it landed at
+0.100.

**The gate passes**, so width-dependent cells stay in round 8. But the honest reading changed twice:

* it is **+0.100, not +0.200** — still the largest clean training-side effect in the campaign, and
  still only ~1.6× the 8-seed wobble of 0.061;
* it is **not a smooth width effect**. Among runs that train successfully the ladder is monotonic
  (0.357 → 0.550 → 0.660 at 384/1536/6144), and what caps it is that wide training *fails* —
  1 of 8 seeds at 1536, 2 of 5 at 6144. The binding constraint is optimisation, not capacity.

**And the process lesson is the sharper one.** I published +0.200 from six of eight *trained* seeds
while two sat unevaluated on disk, one of them visibly sick. That is the same
read-before-the-seeds-land error I spent the day correcting in `d2048`, in `lr9e5`, and in the
`vote5-median` committee count — committed in my own headline number, on the same day, after writing
the rule against it into `docs/measurement-protocol.md` §1.

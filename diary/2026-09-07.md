# Round 9 — a state-space encoder, and the single-frame limit that decides it

> **Read `README.md` first.** `docs/measurement-protocol.md` is binding except where this
> document explicitly overrides it, and every override is named in §6.

## 0. The premise: one archived arm was filed under the wrong lesson

`PiWM-blockcausal` is recorded in `2026-09-06 §0` as *"`block_causal` off everywhere;
`PiWM-blockcausal` scored 0.00, 0.00, 0.00 at n = 3"*, and every round-8 proposal treated that
as **"encoder-side temporal structure fails."**

It is not what happened.

| | `effective_dim` | `rel_mse` | planning SR |
|---|---|---|---|
| `PiWM-blockcausal` | 25.0 – 26.5 (**healthy**) | **0.0012 – 0.0044** | **0.00 ×3** |
| `LpWM-ltv` baseline | 25.9 | 0.0098 | 0.357 |

The arm did not collapse. Its code stayed full-rank and it fit one-step prediction **2–8×
better than the baseline** while planning at exactly zero.

**And it is not simply "low error is bad."** Ranking all 96 arms that have both numbers, the
five lowest `rel_mse` are the five worst planners — but four of them are explained by a dead
code, and one is not:

| arm | `rel_mse` | `effective_dim` | `block_causal` | SR |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `PiWM-drop95` | 0.00000 | **0.00** | false | 0.006 |
| `LpWM-ltv-d2048-hilr` | 0.00000 | **0.00** | false | 0.010 |
| `PiWM-white-dz` | 0.00082 | 14.31 | false | 0.010 |
| `PiWM-white-zt` | 0.00110 | 12.33 | false | 0.013 |
| **`PiWM-blockcausal`** | 0.00155 | **25.04** | **true** | **0.000** |
| `LpWM-ltv` | 0.00919 | 24.10 | false | 0.357 |

A constant code is trivially predictable, so `drop95` and `d2048-hilr` reach zero error by
being rank-0, and the `white-*` pair by halving their rank. **`blockcausal` is the only arm in
the archive with near-perfect fit, a fully healthy full-rank code — 25.04, *above* the
baseline's 24.10 — and exactly zero success.** Pooled across arms, lower error does predict
higher success (ρ = **−0.641**, p < 1e-4), which makes `blockcausal` an outlier against the
archive's own trend rather than an instance of it.

That is a **dissociation**, and it has a mechanical cause:

```
planning/cem.py:77   z_obs_g = self.wm.encode_obs_linked(trans_obs_g)
plan.py:233          obs_g[k] = np.expand_dims(obs_g[k], axis=1)     # time axis = 1
```

**Training encodes clips of T = 3. The goal is encoded as a clip of T = 1.** With
`block_causal`, every frame attends to earlier frames, so `z_goal` — computed with nothing to
attend to — is an object the model never produced during training. The planner then minimises
distance to a point outside its own code distribution. Success 0.00 is the correct outcome of
a well-fit model with a broken target.

**The lesson round 9 is built on:** *any stateful encoder must have a well-defined
single-frame limit, because the goal always is one.* This is why an SSM is the right operator
and attention was the wrong one — an SSM with `s₀ = 0` reduces exactly to a per-frame function,
and attention over a length-1 sequence simply is a different function.

## 1. What the state is *for*

PushT and Wall are both **nearly fully observed** — block, agent, wall and door are all visible
in a single frame. The textbook justification for a temporal encoder (build a belief state for
a POMDP) therefore has almost nothing to work on, and asserting it would repeat the `d_action`
and `effective_dim` mistake: a mechanism the environment cannot exercise.

So round 9 makes the condition it needs, and tests two claims:

**Claim A — denoising.** The SSM integrates the encoder's own per-frame noise. Predicts a small
uniform gain, present at every corruption level including zero.

**Claim B — belief state.** The SSM infers what the current frame does not show. Untestable in
either environment as shipped, so round 9 **manufactures partial observability** with a frame
dropout ladder and tests the *dose-response*: the gain must grow with corruption, and be ~0 at
p = 0.

These make opposite predictions at p = 0, which is what makes the ladder an experiment rather
than a sweep. A flat response refutes B. A response present only at p = 0 refutes B and
supports A. No response anywhere refutes both, cleanly.

## 2. Where the state lives — four placements

All four share one **non-negotiable contract**: `A = 0` reproduces today's encoder exactly.
That is what gives every arm a zero-parameter-difference control *and* a defined `z_goal`.

### 2.0 The trap that must be handled before any of them

**`mup_init_` silently destroys the contract.** `models/mup.py:146-154` unconditionally
re-initialises every `nn.Linear` to `N(0, 1/√fan_in)` with zero bias, and `train.py:1056`
calls it on the encoder. A deliberate `A = 0`, `B = C = I` **does not survive construction**.
The existing "re-apply structural init" pass at `mup.py:168-231` recognises only
`ConditionalBlock` and `LinearDynamicsPredictor`.

```python
if isinstance(m, _LINEARISH):                 # mup.py:146
    std = 1.0 / math.sqrt(_fan_in(m.weight))
    nn.init.normal_(m.weight, mean=0.0, std=std)   # <- clobbers A = 0
```

**Fix:** register the new module in that pass and re-apply `A←0, B←I, C←I` *after*
`mup_init_`. **Verification is an assertion that `enc_A.weight` is all-zero after
construction, not after `__init__`** — the two are different moments and only the first is
the one that ships.

Two further hazards in the same file, both **substring**-matched:

| pattern | file | what it does |
|---|---|---|
| `_INPUT_WEIGHTS` contains `"embed.0.weight"` | `mup.py:55-64` | any submodule whose param name *contains* it is reclassified as an input weight and gets `base_lr` regardless of fan-in |
| `_EMB_KEYS` contains `cls_token`, `pos_embedding` | `mup.py:34` | matching params are re-initialised to `N(0, 0.02)` |

So the new parameters are named **`enc_A` / `enc_Bz` / `enc_C`** and **`scan_*`** — no
collisions. A new `nn.Linear` is otherwise picked up automatically by `mup_fan_in_map`'s
`isinstance` walk and gets the hidden rule `base_lr · base_width / fan_in`. **An unrecognised
module does not raise — it silently gets `base_lr`**, so the printed schema is the only signal.

### 2.1 P1 · `PiWM-enc-ssm` — a temporal state after the per-frame encoder

![P1](assets/2026-09-07/arch-p1-enc-ssm.svg)

Closest to the one mechanism that leaned positive. `models/vit_encoder.py`, ctor kwarg
`enc_ssm=False`; when set, three `nn.Linear(proj_dim, proj_dim, bias=False)` and a
`forward_state(x, T)` that runs **after** the existing per-frame `forward()`, so the ViT is
untouched:

```python
z = self.forward(x)                          # (b*T, P, D) -- unchanged, per-frame
z = rearrange(z, "(b t) p d -> b t p d", t=T)
s = z.new_zeros(z.shape[0], z.shape[2], z.shape[3])
out = []
for t in range(T):                           # T = 3 at train, T = 1 at goal time
    s = self.enc_A(s) + self.enc_Bz(z[:, t])
    out.append(self.enc_C(s))
return rearrange(torch.stack(out, 1), "b t p d -> (b t) p d")
```

`_encode_visual` (`visual_world_model.py:1283-1288`) dispatches to it exactly as it already
dispatches to `forward_temporal` for `block_causal`.

**Control `PiWM-enc-ssm-frozen`:** `enc_A` allocated, zero-init, `requires_grad_(False)`.
Identical parameters, ops and RNG draw; the only difference is whether state can flow.

### 2.2 P2 · `PiWM-enc-ssm-deep` — the same state between ViT blocks

![P2](assets/2026-09-07/arch-p2-deep.svg)

`Transformer.forward` has a hardcoded loop, **but `block_class` is already an injectable ctor
argument** (`infojepa_modules.py:183`) that `ViTEncoder` never passes. The dispatch inside the
loop is what constrains the design:

```python
for block in self.layers:                    # infojepa_modules.py:219-222
    if isinstance(block, Block):
        x = block(x, attn_mask=attn_mask)
    else:
        x = block(x, c, attn_mask=attn_mask)
```

**`StateBlock` must subclass `Block`.** A non-subclass is called with three positional
arguments and dies on arity. It needs `T` to fold `(b, T·L, D) → (b, T, L, D)`; `T` is
`num_hist`, fixed per run, so it is passed at construction and the fold is asserted to divide.

**This is the placement most likely to fail, and that is why it is here.** It is
architecturally closest to `blockcausal` — early mixing — but with the single-frame limit
`blockcausal` lacked. If P2 works, §0's diagnosis is confirmed. If P2 is also ~0, early mixing
is harmful for a reason beyond the goal contract, and P1 is the only viable depth.

### 2.3 P3 · `PiWM-enc-scan` — a bidirectional scan over tokens

![P3](assets/2026-09-07/arch-p3-scan.svg)

Replaces attention over the **257-token** sequence (256 patches + CLS at 224/14) with two
linear sweeps. The drop-in contract, read off `Attention` (`infojepa_modules.py:99-118`):

- `forward(self, x, attn_mask=None) -> (B, L, D)`
- applies **its own** LayerNorm — note `Block` already pre-norms with
  `elementwise_affine=False`, so the path is `LN_noaffine → LN_affine → op`
- honours a `(L, L)` bool mask, `True` = attend
- gates dropout on `self.training`

Bidirectional because patch tokens have no causal order; a one-way scan would impose a
raster-order prior the image does not have. **Requires `FEATURE=patch`** — at `cls`
`num_patches` is 1 and there is nothing to scan, so the arm asserts rather than no-opping.

**Not comparable to attention on parameter count.** `inner_dim = heads × dim_head = 192 ≠
dim = 384`, so attention's `to_out.0` already has fan-in 192 and receives **2× `base_lr`**
under muP. The scan has no heads. Its control is therefore `scan_A` frozen at zero, which
degenerates to a per-token MLP — not the attention block.

**Cost:** a 257-step Python loop per block × 12 blocks. The canary decides whether 8 windows
suffice before the wave launches.

### 2.4 P4 · `PiWM-enc-st-scan` — the two composed

![P4](assets/2026-09-07/arch-p4-st-scan.svg)

P3's spatial operator with P1's temporal head. Ablated by P1 (temporal only) and P3 (spatial
only), both of which run, so a win is attributable rather than mysterious. Both halves reduce
to identity when their `A` is zero, so the arm nests both controls.

## 2A. What the state is *made* to do — three objectives

**P1–P4 change architecture and nothing else.** The state is built, and then trained only by
the objective that already exists: a one-step latent MSE plus RDMReg. **Nothing in the loss
requires the state to carry anything.** That is precisely the failure mode this archive has
recorded twice already:

| arm | what it added | what demanded it be used | result |
|---|---|---|---|
| `PiWM-blockcausal` | cross-frame attention | nothing | **0.00 ×3** |
| `TOKEN_DROP` | tokens dropped per frame | nothing predicts them | a regulariser, not an objective |

So P5–P7 supply the missing demand. All three are **loss terms on the state**, all are
`images and proprio only` — `states.pth` never enters any of them — and all are inert at
weight 0, so each has a bit-identical control.

They are also deliberately *different kinds* of objective: a consistency loss, a regression
loss, and a contrastive loss. If all three fail, "give the state a job" is refuted broadly
rather than for one choice of job.

### 2A.1 P5 · `PiWM-state-consist` — the state must survive a corrupted view

**The idea.** Encode the clip twice: once clean, once through the §3 frame-dropout corruption.
The state from the corrupted view must match the state from the clean view.

```python
s_clean = encoder.states(clip)            # no grad
s_drop  = encoder.states(frame_drop(clip, p))
loss += consist_w * F.mse_loss(s_drop, s_clean.detach())
```

**Why this one.** It turns the observability ladder from an *evaluation* axis into a *training*
signal, and it is the direct operationalisation of Claim A: if the state is a denoiser, this is
the loss that asks it to be one. A per-frame encoder cannot satisfy it — a dropped frame is
zeros and there is nothing else to draw on — so the term is only reducible *by carrying state*.

**Control `PiWM-state-consist-shuf`.** Match `s_drop` against a clean state from a **different
clip in the batch**. Identical magnitude, identical op count, identical corruption; only the
pairing is wrong. A win over this control cannot be "the extra forward pass regularises".

**Falsifier.** If `consist` ≈ `consist-shuf`, the term is doing nothing that pairing explains.

### 2A.2 P6 · `PiWM-state-vel` — the state must decode what one frame cannot show

**The idea.** A single PushT frame shows both poses; the one quantity provably absent from it is
**motion**. So require the state to linearly decode the inter-frame change of the code:

```python
dz = (z[:, 1:] - z[:, :-1]).detach()      # the target is the code's own difference
loss += vel_w * F.mse_loss(self.vel_head(s[:, 1:]), dz)
```

`vel_head` is one `nn.Linear(D, D)`. The target is **self-supervised** — a difference of codes
the encoder already produces — so no privileged state is touched.

**Control `PiWM-state-sum`.** Identical head, identical shapes, but the target is
`z_t + z_{t−1}` instead of `z_t − z_{t−1}`. The **sum is available from either frame alone**;
the **difference is not**. Same magnitude, same parameters, and only the motion content
differs. This is the cleanest control in the round.

**Falsifier.** If `vel` ≈ `sum`, the state is not encoding motion and Claim A's mechanism is
wrong even if the arm happens to score.

### 2A.3 P7 · `PiWM-state-nce` — identify the future, do not regress it

**The idea.** InfoNCE between the state and its own future code, against in-batch negatives:

```python
q = self.nce_q(s[:, :-k])                 # (b, T-k, P, D) -> pooled
kpos = self.nce_k(z[:, k:]).detach()
logits = einsum(q, kpos_all_batch) / tau  # positives on the diagonal
loss += nce_w * F.cross_entropy(logits, arange(b))
```

**Why this one is not a fourth regression.** §0's table is the argument: the four lowest
`rel_mse` arms in the archive are four of the worst planners, because a regression loss **can
be minimised by making the code trivially predictable** — `drop95` and `d2048-hilr` reached
zero error at `effective_dim` 0.00. **A contrastive loss cannot be lowered that way.** If the
code collapses, the negatives become indistinguishable from the positive and the loss goes
*up*. It is the only objective in this round that is structurally collapse-resistant, which is
exactly the pathology §0 is about.

**Control `PiWM-state-nce-shuf`.** Same loss with the positive drawn from a random offset
rather than the true `+k`. Preserves the contrastive form and destroys only the temporal
alignment.

**Falsifier.** If `nce` ≈ `nce-shuf`, the state is not identifying *its own* future.

### 2A.4 What the three share

Each carries a **manipulation check read before the outcome**: P5 must lower the
clean-vs-corrupted state distance; P6 must lower the velocity-probe error; P7 must raise
future-retrieval accuracy above chance. **A term that cannot move its own target is reported
inert, not null** — the rule `docs/measurement-protocol.md §4.1` records after four arms moved
`effective_dim` to 27–28 and planned at 0.02–0.08.

All three attach to **P1's state** by default, because P1 is the cheapest placement with an
exact single-frame limit. Each is one weight, and at 0 the path is bit-identical.

## 3. The observability ladder


A dataset-level corruption applied to the **observation stream only** — never to actions,
never to proprio, and never to the reward or state:

```python
# datasets/img_transforms.py -- applied AFTER the existing transform
if frame_drop > 0 and self.training:
    m = torch.rand(T) < frame_drop        # one draw per frame, per clip
    m[0] = False                          # never drop the anchor frame
    visual[m] = 0.0
```

Three rungs: **p ∈ {0.0, 0.15, 0.35}**. `p = 0` is bit-identical to today and is the control
for the whole ladder.

**Why frame dropout and not occlusion.** A dropped frame creates an inference problem a
stateful encoder can actually solve (carry the last state forward) and a per-frame encoder
provably cannot (it sees zeros). An occluder is confounded with `TOKEN_DROP`, which the archive
already shows is a regulariser rather than an objective.

**Why the anchor frame is never dropped.** `z_src = z_emb[:, :num_hist]` and the planner's
`obs_0`; dropping frame 0 would corrupt the planner's entry point rather than its history.

## 4. Two environments

Every conclusion this campaign has drawn is a **single-environment** result — **882 of 882**
archived configs used `pusht`. `conf/env/wall.yaml`, `datasets/wall_dset.py`,
`env/wall/wall_env_wrapper.py` and `conf/plan_wall.yaml` all exist and all import cleanly, and
the 55 GB dataset is on disk. It has never been run.

Wall is 1,920 episodes × 50 steps, 2-D dot, 2-D actions, with `door_locations` and
`wall_locations` varying per episode — so it also supplies a **compositional generalisation**
axis the wrapper already exposes (`exclude_door_train`, `only_door_val`).

`states.pth` is present in the wall dataset. **The standing rule holds: images and proprio
only. `states.pth` may be read for measurement and diagnostics and must never enter a training
loss.**

## 5. The grid

Two families, priced separately, because they answer different questions.

**Placements (P1–P4)** — where the state lives.

| factor | levels |
|---|---|
| placement | P1, P2, P3, P4 |
| arm | treatment + frozen-`A` control |
| ladder | p = 0.0, 0.15, 0.35 |
| environment | pusht, wall |
| seeds | **5** |

`4 × 2 × 3 × 2 × 5 = 240`, plus an unmodified baseline per (rung × env) — `3 × 2 × 5 = 30` —
for **270 runs**, ≈ 2,400 GPU-h.

**Objectives (P5–P7)** — what the state is made to do. All attach to **P1**, so the placement
is held fixed and the objective is the only thing varying.

| factor | levels |
|---|---|
| objective | P5 consist, P6 vel, P7 nce |
| arm | treatment + its own shuffled/inert control |
| ladder | p = 0.0, 0.35 (P5 needs corruption to mean anything) |
| environment | pusht |
| seeds | **5** |

`3 × 2 × 2 × 5 = 60` runs, ≈ 530 GPU-h.

**Total ≈ 330 runs, ≈ 2,900 GPU-h.** P5–P7 are ~18 % of the round and are the only part that
tests whether the state can be *made* useful rather than merely *available*.

## 6. Measurement — one metric, and where this overrides the protocol

**The only metric that decides anything is CEM planning success rate against the baseline**,
paired on shared seeds, fixed evaluation scheme, `goal_H = 5`, `max_iter = 10`, 50 episodes.
No diagnostic is a target. `rel_mse`, `effective_dim` and the probes are recorded for mechanism
only and cannot promote or demote an arm — `blockcausal` is the reason: it had the archive's
best `rel_mse` and the archive's worst success.

**Override, stated explicitly:** `docs/measurement-protocol.md §1` sets a minimum of **n = 8**
and **n = 20** for effects below +0.10. Round 9 runs **n = 5** by instruction. The consequence
is arithmetic, not opinion: at the healthy-arm paired sd of 0.169, the MDE at n = 5 is
**≈ 0.21** — larger than *every* effect round 8 produced, positive or negative, except the four
catastrophic ones. Two specific risks follow:

1. **Anything real but modest is invisible.** `ssm`'s +0.066 would not be detectable at n = 5.
2. **Anything that looks large is probably not.** `st-ssm` read +0.083 at n = 6 and **+0.000**
   at n = 8; at n = 5 it would have been reported as a win.

The mitigation that costs nothing: **the ladder is a within-arm dose-response**, and a monotone
trend across three rungs is far more informative at n = 5 than any single rung's contrast. Read
the slope, not the cell.

### 7.1 The audit that makes "no overlap" checkable

`analysis/fig_audit.py` is new. The previous `round8_t_figs.audit()` measured only `<text>`, so
it was blind to 1,632 rects, 489 paths and 75 circles — every "nothing overlaps" claim in
rounds 6–8 rested on it. `audit_all` adds text×shape, shape×shape and off-canvas for drawn
marks, and distinguishes **containment** (a panel holding a frame — normal, and what makes a
naive pairwise check useless here) from **partial overlap**.

Defects it caught while these were built, none of which the old check could see: a footer strip
overflowing its panel, an `✗` stamped inside the `A = 0` box instead of on the wire it strikes,
two captions sitting inside `RDMReg`, and axis labels landing on a frame's tokens. It also
caught **a bug in itself**: parsing `d=` as flat x,y pairs reads an arc's
`A rx ry rot large-arc sweep x y` flag triple as a coordinate, and every dome here is an arc,
so three phantom off-canvas marks appeared per figure until the parser became command-aware.

**And one class it structurally cannot catch**, found only by reading the renders back: the
attention fan-in in P3 was seven segments drawn at a single `y`, so they overlaid into one flat
line — geometrically valid, visually empty. Both checks are required.

## 8. Order of work

1. **The `mup_init_` fix and its assertion** (§2.0). Nothing else is meaningful until `A = 0`
   survives construction, because every arm's control depends on it.
2. **The frame-dropout transform**, with bit-identity at p = 0.
3. **P1 + its frozen control**, canary, then the pusht ladder. P1 is the cheapest placement and
   the carrier for every objective in §2A.
4. **P5, P6, P7 on P1.** Deliberately *before* P2–P4: if giving the state a job does nothing at
   the cheapest placement, moving the state deeper or wider is unlikely to rescue it, and the
   round can stop having spent ~900 GPU-h instead of ~2,900.
5. **Wall end-to-end on the baseline alone** — it has never run; prove the env before spending
   a factorial on it.
6. **P2, then P3, then P4** — P2 is the cheap test of §0's diagnosis, P3 is the expensive build.

## 9. What would falsify the round

* **P1 flat across all three rungs** ⇒ both claims dead; state buys nothing here.
* **P2 ≈ 0.00 like `blockcausal`** ⇒ the goal-contract diagnosis in §0 is wrong, and early
  mixing is harmful for a different reason.
* **Gain at p = 0 but not rising with p** ⇒ Claim B (belief state) refuted, Claim A supported.
* **P4 ≤ max(P1, P3)** ⇒ the two axes do not compose, and the ST framing dies.
* **P5 ≈ its shuffled control** ⇒ the consistency term is an extra forward pass, nothing more.
* **P6 ≈ `state-sum`** ⇒ the state is not encoding motion, and Claim A's mechanism is wrong
  even if the arm scores.
* **P7 ≈ `nce-shuf`** ⇒ the state is not identifying its own future.
* **All of P5–P7 flat** ⇒ the strongest negative available here: the state cannot be made
  useful by any of a consistency, a regression or a contrastive objective, and "add temporal
  capacity to the representation" is closed for this architecture rather than for one term.

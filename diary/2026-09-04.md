### Two claims of mine the audit falsified outright

**"O2 keeps ESS = 1.0, unlike T3's sample reweighting."** False. Dividing per-sample by a
per-sample scalar *is* sample reweighting — that is the definition. Measured on the 1536 real
validation samples in `assets/incr_stats_lpwm_ltv_s3.json`:

| | ESS/N | weight span |
|---|---|---|
| **V1-incr / O2 as I proposed it** | **0.063** | 3884× |
| T3 `contact` (γ=1.0) | 0.38 | — |
| uniform | 1.000 | 1× |

My proposal had an effective sample size **six times worse** than the arm whose *shuffled control*
alone cost −0.345.

**"A contracted rollout carries no action information, so an inverse model prevents contraction."**
Inverted. `PiWM-actinfo-cond` — an action-information arm — has **h8/h1 = 1.02**, the signature of
a constant. The untreated baseline sits at **19.4** and is not contracting. *The
action-information objective is what produced the contraction I proposed it to prevent.*

Neither error was subtle, and neither would have surfaced without checking. That is the whole
argument for doing this before spending GPU-hours.

## 2. The screen: two design targets were collapse detectors

While writing the plan I found what looked like the ideal target. `h8/h1` — how much rollout error
grows from one step to eight — spans 12.4 on the campaign's best arm to 24.6 on a near-worst one.
Not at a floor, unlike the Gramian that made P4 a null by construction.

Then I measured it against planning:

```
Spearman(h8/h1, CEM)                      = +0.558   p = 8e-28
partial, removing rho and rel_mse         = -0.017   p = 0.77
healthy only (rel_mse < 0.05), n = 213    = +0.002
```

**Another collapse detector, exactly like `d_action`.** A quantity that separates dead models from
live ones correlates with everything, because dead models plan at zero. Killed before a line was
written, for zero GPU.

So the test became a tool. `analysis/screen_objective.py` asks of any candidate: the raw Spearman
(the number that fools you), the rank-partial with ρ and `rel_mse` removed against a permutation
null (the number that matters), the same restricted to predictors that actually predict (a cut,
reported as a robustness check and never as the headline), and binned quartile means — because a
partial correlation cannot see a non-monotone relation, which is exactly what `d_action` turned
out to be.

![The objective screen](assets/2026-09-04/objective-screen.png)

| candidate | raw | partial | monotone |
|---|---|---|---|
| **`jacc/S_model` — support dissimilarity** | −0.769 | **−0.549** | **yes** |
| `sparsity/effective_dim` | +0.487 | +0.338 | no |
| `sparsity/effective_dim_pred` | +0.609 | +0.331 | no |
| `jacc/S_world` | −0.035 | +0.315 | no |
| `jacc/churn_model` | −0.375 | −0.056 | — |
| `h8/h1` rollout growth | +0.558 | −0.017 | — |
| `h1` rollout error | −0.552 | −0.090 | — |
| **`err/rel_mse`** *(self-null control)* | −0.684 | **+0.003** | — |

`rel_mse` is included as a **self-null control**: it is one of the covariates, so it must screen to
zero or the estimator is broken. It screens to +0.003.

### What the screen endorsed

`S_model = 1 − soft_Jaccard(ẑ, z)` is **support** dissimilarity — *which* units fire — as opposed
to `rel_mse`'s magnitude error. On a code at ρ ≈ 0.45 those are different quantities, and the
partial confirms it carries information `rel_mse` does not. Over healthy predictors it is monotone:
0.407 → 0.365 → 0.249 → 0.058.

And `soft_jaccard` appears **zero times** in `models/`. It has only ever been logged as a
diagnostic; nothing has ever optimised it. **It is a screen-validated, previously untargeted
objective — the first this campaign has had.**

Note the three amber rows. `effective_dim`, `effective_dim_pred` and `S_world` all survive the
partial and are all **non-monotone** — they have an interior optimum, so "raise it" is not a
direction. That is the `d_action` shape, and it is why item 4 of the screen exists.

*Caveat, stated before the arm runs:* the Jaccard is scale-invariant in a way MSE is not, so a
model could lower `S` by inflating the code's magnitude uniformly. The arm logs the code norm every
epoch so that is visible rather than inferred.

## 3. Why every round-6 arm is a grid

Six of the nine round-3/4/5 proposals were **single-shot** on their strength knob: V1, V2, V3, P4,
T6, T4. The consequences are on record:

- **V1 has no strength knob at all.** Its ε is the literal `1e-4` at `visual_world_model.py:1091`,
  **414× below** the median increment of 4.1e-2. The diary's own verdict: *"V1 as run is a test of
  ε = 1e-4, and ε = 1e-4 is wrong… a rerun has not been done."*
- **T6's K was never varied**, and T6 is the one direction §16.8 leaves open.
- **T4 would have died on its primary setting.** Gate: `val/rho_k > 0.6`. `vp` (TD, the spec's
  primary) measures **0.443** and fails; `vp-mc` measures **0.715** and passes. Only the variant
  grid saved it — and T5/V5/V4 must therefore build on `vp-mc`, not `vp`.

So: **full grid at 8 seeds**, and each grid brackets its failure mode around a *measured* quantity
rather than sampling uniformly. No screening stage, because **no early-epoch metric has ever been
validated against final CEM here** — the campaign has four documented retractions from mid-training
reads.

Measured cost from the real archive: **~7.3 GPU-h per (arm, seed)** — 6.6 h training across four
chained windows plus 0.68 h eval. One setting × 8 seeds ≈ **58 GPU-h / 40 jobs**.

### R6 · support agreement

![R6](assets/2026-09-04/arch-r6-support.svg)

The figure carries the argument as a worked pair rather than as prose. Against a target
`[0, 1.0, 0, 0.8, 0, 0]`: case A `[0, 1.4, 0, 1.1, 0, 0]` has the right support and overshooting
magnitudes; case B `[0.25, 1.0, 0.25, 0.8, 0.25, 0.25]` has exact magnitudes and four spurious
units firing. **Both have identical squared error, 0.152.** Their S differs — 0.280 against
0.357. That is the information `rel_mse` cannot see and the screen's −0.549 partial detects.
*(Those three numbers are computed from the drawn vectors; every number in the value strip is
measured.)*

The caveat is drawn too: the Jaccard is scale-invariant in a way MSE is not, so a model could
lower S by inflating the code uniformly. The arm logs the code norm every epoch.

**First finished runs — the objective works, and the caveat that fires is a different one**
*(2026-09-04, n = 1/1/2, far too early for a verdict; this is a guard reading, not a result).*

| `SUPPORT_W` | n | `d_action_over_scale` | vs baseline | `rel_mse` | `S_model` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.03 | 1 | 0.568 | 1.03× | 0.0072 | 0.0536 |
| 0.1  | 1 | 0.329 | 0.60× | 0.0125 | 0.0358 |
| 0.3  | 2 | 0.207 | **0.38×** | 0.044 – 0.100 | 0.0335 – 0.0414 |
| baseline `LpWM-ltv` | 16 | 0.549 (probe) | — | 0.0092 | 0.082 |

Three quantities move monotonically with the dose, which is what makes this worth recording at
n = 1: **`S_model` falls** (0.082 → 0.054 → 0.036 → 0.034), so the objective is doing exactly what
it was built to do; but **action sensitivity falls with it** (1.03× → 0.60× → 0.38×) and
**`rel_mse` rises** (0.0072 → 0.0125 → up to 0.100, an order of magnitude above baseline).

The scale caveat above is *not* what fired — the code norm is behaving. What fired is the
`d_action` guard: at `SUPPORT_W = 0.3`, **both** finished seeds sit at 0.212 and 0.201, below the
baseline probe's own **minimum** of 0.222, not merely below the 0.2746 threshold. That is outside
baseline variation rather than inside it.

So the early shape is that support agreement is **purchasable, but priced in action sensitivity**.
If it holds up, the grid has already earned its keep: `SUPPORT_W = 0.03` buys a third of the
`S_model` reduction at **no** measurable cost on either guard, and a single-setting run at 0.3
would have been read as the objective failing rather than as the weight being too high. This is
the case the round was designed around — see §3 on why every arm is a grid.

**Falsification watch.** The screen endorsed `S_model` because it ordered CEM success among
*healthy* predictors. If the 0.3 arm lowers `S_model` while planning badly, that is not a
refutation of the screen — it is the arm leaving the healthy region the screen was measured on.
The contrast that matters is 0.03 and 0.1 against their `SUPPORT_W = 0` control, at n = 8.

### R3 · action-space SAM

![R3](assets/2026-09-04/arch-r3-sam.svg)

The guard is drawn in crimson because this objective's unconstrained minimiser *is* the collapse
mode: a predictor that ignores the action inside the ρ-ball has `d_action = 0` and a perfect SAM
loss. `d_action` is therefore a first-class logged metric for this arm, not an afterthought.

**The guard, stated in the units it is actually checked in.** An earlier draft read "collapsed if
`d_action` falls more than 2× below the baseline's 0.549". That phrasing is a trap, and it caught
me: **0.549 is `causal/d_action_over_scale`, not `causal/d_action`.** The baseline's raw `d_action`
median is **0.3535**. Comparing a raw value against the normalised threshold understates every arm
by ~1.55× and manufactures collapses that are not there. The guard is:

> collapsed ⇔ `causal/d_action_over_scale` < **0.2746** = half the `LpWM-ltv` probe median
> (0.5491, n=16, `assets/d_action_probe.json`).

Two further cautions on the comparator. The baseline arms **log no `causal/d_action` at all** — the
diagnostic postdates them — so the probe is the *only* valid source for the threshold, and it must
be the probe on both sides or neither. And the baseline's own probe spread is **[0.222, 0.629]**,
which straddles the threshold: a single seed below 0.2746 is inside baseline variation, so the
guard is a per-arm judgement across seeds, not a per-seed kill switch.

**First five finished runs, all healthy** (2026-09-04): `sam-r0p03` s4 0.423, `sam-r0p1` s6 0.480,
s9 0.448, s7 0.385, s10 0.376 — **0.68–0.87× baseline, no breach**, with `rel_mse` 0.009–0.014,
i.e. at baseline quality. The collapse mode the guard exists to catch has not appeared at ρ ≤ 0.1.

### R4 · V1 completed

![R4](assets/2026-09-04/arch-r4-eps.svg)

The ε ladder is the point of the figure: 1e-4 as shipped (ESS 0.063, span 3884×, and the arm
scored −0.383), then 1e-3 → 0.242, 1e-2 → 0.591, and 4.1e-2 — the measured median — → 0.813.
Note where that leaves the experiment: the window between *ESS-collapsed* and *inert* is narrow,
because ε → ∞ recovers plain MSE exactly.

**ESS is seed-dependent, and both measurements are kept.** Those figures come from
`LpWM-ltv` **s3** (1536 validation windows, median increment 0.0415). Calibrating the knob on
**s0** gives a median of 0.0771 and therefore ESS 0.220 at the same ε = 1e-4. The median
increment differs 1.9× between two seeds of the same arm, so "the ESS at the shipped ε" is a
property of the checkpoint, not of the objective. Neither number was allowed to overwrite the
other; `conf/train_rdmreg.yaml` records both. It does not change the reading — 0.063 and 0.220
are a 16× and a 4.5× loss of effective batch respectively — and §7 reports what the arms
actually did in training, which is the number that settles it.

**First finished run, and it changes what R4 is** *(2026-09-04, n = 1 — a mechanism reading, not
a result).* `incr-eps0p041` s7 finished. Set beside its own 1-epoch canary:

| | `rel_mse` | `d_action_over_scale` | `l0_frac_pred` | ESS |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| canary, 1 epoch | **0.5176** | 0.176 | 0.821 | 0.582 |
| finished, 2 epochs | **0.1110** | 0.135 | 0.610 | **0.790** |
| round-5 V1 at ε = 1e-4 (n = 8) | — | — | **0.000** on 7/8 | **0.063** |
| baseline `LpWM-ltv` | 0.0092 | 0.549 | — | — |

Two things follow, and they point in opposite directions.

**The ε hypothesis is being confirmed at the mechanism level.** R4 exists because V1 was killed by
a hard-coded ε = 1e-4 that sat 414× below the median increment, and the diagnosis was that this
destroys the effective batch. Raising ε to the measured median moves **ESS 0.063 → 0.790**, within
3 % of the 0.813 predicted from the increment distribution — the calibration was right. And the
signature failure is gone: round-5's V1 drove `l0_frac_pred` to **exactly 0.000** on 7 of 8 seeds,
an all-zero predictor code, where this arm holds **0.610**. The reweighting pathology was
genuinely an ε problem, not an idea problem.

**And the arm still fails a guard.** `d_action_over_scale` = 0.135 is **0.25×** baseline, far below
the 0.2746 threshold and below the baseline's own minimum of 0.222. `rel_mse` 0.111 is 12× the
baseline's 0.0092. Fixing the effective batch did not buy a good model.

**The canary would have been wrong.** This arm read `rel_mse` 0.518 at one epoch — above the
pre-registered death condition — and 0.111 at two, a 4.7× improvement. Three of the four R4
canaries exceeded 0.5 and the arms were nearly withheld on that basis. **Applying an
end-of-training threshold to a 1-epoch read would have killed an arm that is not dead**, and
would have destroyed the closure R4 exists to produce. The death condition is checked at end of
training, on finished runs, by `scripts/watch/w25_watch.sh` — not on canaries.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `incr-eps0p001` | 3 | 0.0000 | **1/3** | **0.0000** | 0.00× | **2 of 3** |
| `incr-eps0p01` | 2 | 0.5167 | **1/2** | 0.0286 | 0.05× | 0 |
| `incr-eps0p041` | 3 | 0.2875 | **1/3** | 0.1346 | 0.25× | 0 |
| `incr-eps0p041-clip10` | 1 | 0.0656 | 0/1 | 0.1684 | 0.31× | 0 |

**Every finished R4 seed sits below the baseline probe's minimum on `d_action_over_scale`**, and at
ε = 1e-3 two of three are *exactly* 0.000 — the predictor has stopped responding to the action at
all. That is V1's failure, not a repaired version of it: round-5 V1 drove `l0_frac_pred` to 0.000,
this drives `d_action` to 0.000. The collapse moved metrics; it did not go away.

What survives from the retracted reading is narrow and worth keeping: **s7 specifically** did reach
ESS 0.790 against the 0.813 predicted from the increment distribution, so the *ESS calibration
arithmetic* was right. What does not survive is the inference from that one seed to "the
reweighting pathology was an ε problem, not an idea problem". With 1 death in 3 and `d_action` at
0.25×, the honest statement is that **ε controls the severity of the collapse without preventing
it** — the family is dose-ordered (0.000 → 0.029 → 0.135 → 0.168 as ε rises) and every point on
the ladder is still collapsed.

The canary point stands on its own evidence and is unaffected: s7 did go 0.518 → 0.111, so a
1-epoch read genuinely cannot be judged against an end-of-training threshold. But note what that
argument does and does not license. It licenses *letting the arms run*. It does not license
reading their result early, which is exactly what I then did. Both errors have the same root, and
§3's rule — no contrast read below n = 8 — was written for the second one too.

### R5 · ensemble M-curve

![R5](assets/2026-09-04/arch-r5-mcurve.svg)

**R2's construction is the part worth explaining.** Sample action sequences from the CEM proposal
(`randn·σ + μ`) and require the K-step jump to agree with the K chained one-step predictions.
*Both sides are the model's own outputs*, so no ground truth is needed for actions no simulator
ever rolled — which is what removes O5's original dependency on the collapsed action-information
family. It is defined on the planner's *query* distribution rather than on a model property, and
M4 already measured the failure it targets: CEM's predicted improvement beats the realised one on
**38 %** of baseline episodes and **6.7 %** under oracle dynamics.

**R4 is here to be settled, not to win.** It sits in the reweighting family §16.8 declares closed,
and ε → the median approaches inertness (ESS/N: 0.242 / 0.591 / 0.813 across the grid, against
0.063 as shipped). But V1 is the campaign's canonical *killed by one constant*, and leaving it
unresolved is the exact thing this round is against. If no ε beats the baseline, the idea is wrong
rather than its constant, and the family closes for good.

**R5 is not a scale proposal.** The user has been explicit that scale is not the direction. The
*shape* of the M-curve is a measurement: saturation at M = 3 says the members' errors are
correlated and points at a common-mode defect; a curve that keeps rising says they are independent
and the single-model ceiling is real. Either answer constrains round 7.

## 5. `spectral.py`, run for the first time — and it corrects two things

`analysis/spectral.py` computes the predictor's spectral radius, its controllability Gramian and
`A_aug^k B_aug` from checkpoint weights alone — no data, no GPU. §11 said it *"can refute this
round's own premise"*. **It had never been executed**: no output JSON, no slurm log, no number in
any diary. It has now run over the whole archive — 412 checkpoints, 32 of which have no linear
core (`lie`, `action_linear`, `ar_adaln`, `vit`).

### 5.1 Every learned predictor is expansive. Round 4's stated reason for dropping the spectral penalty was false.

```
spectral radius rho:  median 17.7   range [1.05, 145.4]
fraction with rho >= 1:  1.000        <- all 399 non-canary checkpoints
```

Round 4 dropped a `(r−1)²` spectral penalty with this reason: *"PushT is dissipative — `r < 1` is
the model being **right**. Damaging the model to flatter a diagnostic."* **Not one checkpoint has
`r < 1`.** The premise was never measured, and it is false. The environment may well be
dissipative; the learned latent dynamics are not.

That said, the penalty should still not be built, and now for a measured reason rather than an
assumed one: `Spearman(ρ, CEM) = +0.028` over 309 evaluated runs. **ρ does not predict planning**,
so it fails the §2 screen like everything else that is a property of the model in isolation.

### 5.2 The controllability finding, and a correction to `2026-09-03.md` §12

The action reaches almost none of the latent space. Over all non-canary checkpoints the
controllability matrix has median **rank/dim = 0.32**, and **34 % of checkpoints are below 0.05** —
the baseline `LpWM-ltv` sits at rank 255/1152 and `LpWM-ltv-d2048`, the campaign's *best* single
model, at **18/6144**.

This forced a correction to §12's reading of P4, which I had got wrong. On the two `linear_var`
arms, where the linearisation is exact rather than approximate:

| | `PiWM-ctrb` | `LpWM-linvar` (control) |
|---|---|---|
| log₁₀ cond(W_c) | **0.60** | **10.18** |
| rank / dim | 1152 / 1152 | 1119 / 1152 |

I had written that P4 was a null *"by construction — the Gramian was already close to isotropic"*,
reading its **converged** `ctrb_loss` of 0.033 as a pre-treatment value. It is post-treatment. The
penalty in fact drove the Gramian **nine and a half orders of magnitude**, to near-perfect
isotropy and full rank, and planning still did not move (+0.015 [−0.07, +0.10]).

**P4 is therefore a much stronger result than the diary recorded**: an interventional null. It
rules out reachability-conditioning as a direction, rather than merely failing to test it. §12 now
carries the correction at the point of use.

### 5.3 What this does to R3 and R4's neighbours

Nothing in round 6 targets ρ or the Gramian, which is now the right call for a measured reason.
It also removes a temptation: `log10 cond(W_c)` has `Spearman(cond, CEM) = +0.473` raw, which
looks like a strong signal and — given every other example in §2 — is almost certainly another
collapse detector. It has not been put through the screen because nothing proposes to optimise it,
and it should be if anyone ever does.

## 6. What the audit means for the record

Two entries in `2026-09-03.md` should be read with this one:

- **§12b** already caught `d_action` being wrong by ~2900×, and §8 is flagged superseded because
  its population excluded every high-CEM arm.
- **§14 rule 4** — *"measure the baseline's value of a quantity before building nine arms to change
  it"* — is now enforced by a script rather than by intention. `d_action` and `h8/h1` would both
  have been caught by it.

The pattern across five rounds is consistent enough to name: **nearly every metric that is a
property of the model in isolation turns out to be a collapse detector.** `rel_mse`, ρ, `eff_dim`,
`d_action`, `h8/h1`, `h1`, support churn — all of them separate dead from live and then go flat.
`S_model` is the first exception found, and it is an exception about *structure* (which units fire)
rather than *magnitude*. Whether that survives being optimised is R6's question.

#### The instrument could not resolve most of what round 6 asked it

Median sd of the paired difference for a **retrained** arm is **0.147**, giving an MDE at n = 8 of
**0.150**. The between-arm sd of the entire healthy population is **0.099**.

> **A retrained-arm contrast in this campaign cannot detect anything smaller than the whole
> spread of the population it is sampling.**

So round 6's five nulls — R3 SAM at all three ρ, R6 at w = 0.03, R2's `cem-vs-data` — are
**uninformative below ≈ ±0.13**, not evidence of no effect. The only round-6 results this design
could ever have seen are the large negatives (−0.26 to −0.39). §7.2's falsification of R6 is one
of them and is safe; the nulls should not be read as "this does nothing".

The reason is measurable and was never checked: the **training seed** is the dominant axis of
variation. On the `PiWM-soloM` 5 × 5 model × block matrix, **SS(model) = 92 %** against
SS(block) = 1 %, with five seeds of one config spanning **0.180 → 0.600**. Training-seed sd is
0.144 against a binomial sd of 0.043, and because the effect is an arm × seed *interaction*
(mean cross-arm correlation of demeaned seed profiles 0.150), **pairing removes only ~7 % of it**.

#### What my first reanalysis got wrong

It read the strongly-negative arms as *training failures*. Three independent checks say no:

* A training-side definition of "dead" (`rel_mse ≥ 0.5`) gives **ΔP = 0.00** for `consist` w0.1
  and w0.3, `support` w0.3, `contact-shuf` and `drop95`. Only `incr` ε=1e-3 shows real collapse.
* `consist-w0p3`'s eight seeds sit inside the baseline's range on `rel_mse` (0.0096–0.0179),
  `effective_dim` (18.9–20.6) **and** `val/loss` simultaneously — and score 0.005 officially,
  0.255 on the block. **A planning-and-scoring result, exactly as §7.0 recorded it.**
* Five of the six arms show **no within-arm seed heterogeneity** (bootstrap LRT p = 0.08–0.63).
  They are uniformly at the floor, not a mixture of live and dead seeds.

And the decomposition that produced the claim is not identified at all — see `README` §7.

#### One thing the exercise did buy

`sparsity/effective_dim == 0` is the **only perfect predictor of SR ≈ 0** in the archive: 24 runs
across 12 arms, every evaluated one at SR ≤ 0.04. The pre-registered gate `rel_mse ≥ 0.5` cannot
see this, because a constant-output model has `rel_mse ≈ 0`. Both should be checked.

should be kept — it is cheap and it removes a real failure mode — but it must be re-labelled: it

*(`analysis/rollout_snr.json` + `rollout_snr_t6.json`, 2026-09-04. This section records a
correlation I found, believed for about twenty minutes, and then killed with a probe.)*

Everything else tonight said latent accuracy is not the lever, so I looked for the one form of

| screen | ρ(K5 err, CEM) | p |
|---|---|---|
| all seeds | **−0.786** | 0.0015 |
| excluding the dead seed (CEM = 0) | **−0.727** | 0.0074 |

which on its own is *not* significant. Better 5-step rollout, better planning. It is the first
rollout quantity in this campaign to clear that bar, and it is measured **within one arm**, so
it is not a cross-arm collapse detector.

**Then I checked whether the intervention existed. It did, and it is negative.**

| arm | median K5 err / target var | CEM |
|---|---|---|
| `PiWM-overshoot5` — trains the K-step **chain** | **0.0541** *(3.7× better than baseline)* | **0.193** |
| `LpWM-ltv` — trains 1 step | 0.2018 | **0.357** |

`overshoot5` **reduces the very quantity by 3.7×** and plans **−0.200 [−0.387, −0.013]** worse
(§16.10). The correlation is real and the intervention on it is harmful.

Read the three rows together and the dissociation is about as clean as this campaign can produce:
**across a 33× range of 5-step rollout error, planning does not follow.** The best planner has
*intermediate* error; the arm with the best rollout and the arm with the worst rollout plan the
same, and both plan worse than the baseline that is mediocre at it. Not even monotone.

*(The `jump5` row also explains its own result mechanically. It trains the K-step jump, but
`visual_world_model.rollout` advances the planner **one step at a time**, chaining — so the
object `jump5` optimises is not the object CEM uses, and its chained rollout is worse than
predicting the mean. That is a train/plan mismatch, not a failure of the jump idea, and it is
worth separating from T6's own contrast, which is `jump5` against `overshoot5` and is a

**This is the third time today, and the pattern is now the finding.**

| quantity | how it was selected | what the intervention did |
|---|---|---|
| `d_action`, `h8/h1` | raw correlation | caught by the screen before any arm was built |
| `S_model` | **passed** the screen: partial −0.549, monotone, previously untargeted | **−0.260** at a healthy dose (§7.2) |

Every observational correlate of planning success this campaign has found — screened with
progressively more care each time — has failed under intervention. The screen is worth keeping
because it is free and it does reject the crudest failures. But **its endorsement carries no
information about what happens when you optimise the quantity**, and that is now demonstrated
three times rather than argued once.

The operational rule this yields is cheap and should be standing: **before proposing a target,
search the archive for an arm that already moves it.** Here that took one probe over eight
checkpoints and turned a round-7 proposal into a closed question.

### 7.4 The cls latent cannot represent the task's key variable — and patch tokens can

*(`analysis/latent_probe.py`, already run and sitting in `assets/latent_probe_columns*.json`.
Ridge-decode the block pose from a single FROZEN frame — no dynamics involved. Privileged state
is used here for **measurement only**, never in a loss. The headline is median absolute angular
error in degrees; `const_err_ang_deg` is the best achievable CONSTANT prediction, which is the
bound that matters because PushT's T settles into a canonical orientation and the angle is
strongly non-uniform.)*

| arm | feature | n | median angular error | vs the constant bound (14.51°) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **`PiWM-columns`** | **patch** | 13 | **9.58°** | **−4.9°, decisively below** |
| `LpWM-ltv-d2048` | cls, 5× width | 6 | 14.64° | +0.1°, **at the bound** |
| `LpWM-ltv` (baseline) | cls | 16 | **15.72°** | **+1.2°, WORSE than the bound** |
| `PiWM-drop95` | patch, 95 % dropped | 7 | 16.01° | +1.5°, worse than the bound |

**The baseline's latent carries no usable orientation information whatsoever.** At 15.72° it is
*worse than predicting the block's canonical angle every single time*. PushT is a task about
aligning a T-shaped block; the representation every one of ~120 contrasts was built on cannot
see the variable the task is about.

**Width does not fix it.** `d2048` is five times wider and lands at 14.64° — exactly the
constant bound. Capacity is not the constraint.

**Patch tokens do fix it.** `PiWM-columns` — which is a *clean single-factor change*, its arm spec
is byte-identical to `LpWM-ltv` (`ARMS[PiWM-columns]="ltv 1.0 5e-4"`) with only
`encoder.feature: cls → patch` differing — reaches **9.58°**, the only arm in the archive that
beats the bound.

**And the dose-response points the same way, with a caveat added 2026-09-05.** `drop95` is
*also* patch-feature but discards 95 % of its tokens, and it falls back to 16.01° — at the bound,
like cls. That suggests it is not "patch is a better setting" but that **the tokens are where the
orientation lives**.

> ⚠ **`drop95` is a weak endpoint and should not carry this argument alone.** Four of its seven
> seeds are in *total representational collapse* — `effective_dim` = 0.0, `dead_unit_frac` = 1.0
> — so its probe value is what any fully-collapsed encoder would give, not a clean measurement of
> "patch tokens minus 95 % of them". This is exactly what round 7's **S1** exists to fix: a
> `TOKEN_DROP` ladder at 0.25/0.50/0.75/0.90, where the intermediate points can be checked for
> collapse before being read.

#### Why this is the lead round 7 should follow

Set it beside the rest of the record. Every intervention this campaign has run changed the
*objective* on a *fixed* representation — `feature=cls`, a **single 384-d token for the whole
image** — and:

* ~120 contrasts produced exactly one positive, and that one (consensus) is not a world-model
  result at all (`2026-09-03` §16.9).
* Three separate observational targets passed screening and then failed under intervention
  (§7.3), the last of them across a **33× range** of the quantity.
* Latent accuracy does not predict planning; independent errors average away at the theoretical
  rate and planning does not move (§7.5).

All of that is what you would expect **if the representation is missing the task's controlled
variable**: no loss defined on that latent can recover information the latent does not contain,
and improving prediction of an orientation-blind code is exactly the null result the campaign
kept producing.

The CEM evidence points the same way and is the best non-consensus signal available:
`PiWM-columns` scores **+0.072 [−0.064, +0.207] at n = 12** — a positive-leaning null, and the
largest n of any treated arm. `PiWM-patchdecode`, also patch, is **+0.140 [−0.047, +0.327]**.
Neither clears the floor; both are the only treated arms that lean the right way.

**What I am NOT claiming.** I also computed a rank test of "patch arms vs cls arms" over the whole
campaign (3 vs 46 arms, p = 0.0065) and am deliberately **not** reporting it as evidence: it
compares three patch arms against forty-six cls arms most of which carry actively harmful
objectives, so it measures the harm of those objectives, not the feature. The single-factor
contrast — `columns` vs `LpWM-ltv` — is the honest one, and it is a **null at n = 12**. The strong
claim here is about the *probe*, which is a direct measurement and needs no contrast at all.

### 7.5 The members' errors are independent — and averaging them away buys nothing

*(`analysis/residual_corr.py`, new. 16 `LpWM-ltv` checkpoints, one fixed validation batch,
no training and no planner. Run: `python analysis/residual_corr.py --arm LpWM-ltv`.)*

§16.9 of `2026-09-03.md` narrowed the campaign's only positive to this: consensus beats its
members' **mean** by +0.228 and its members' **best** by +0.014 (null). An ensemble that beats the
average but not the best is doing **variance reduction**, and classical theory says exactly how
far that can go. For M members with mean pairwise error correlation ρ̄,

    Var_ens / Var_member  =  ρ̄ + (1 − ρ̄)/M      →  ρ̄   as M → ∞

so the achievable gain is capped by ρ̄ and nothing else. The measured M-curve (0.393 → 0.430 →
0.583 → 0.608 at M = 1, 2, 3, 5) is flattening, which *predicts a large ρ̄* — members making the
same mistakes. That was the hypothesis. It is wrong.

| measured on 16 seeds | value | if errors were independent |
|---|---|---|
| mean pairwise error correlation ρ̄ | **+0.0010** [−0.015, +0.016] | 0 |
| ‖mean residual over 16‖ / mean‖residual‖ | **0.2599** | 0.2500 = 1/√16 |
| ratio observed / independent | **1.040** | 1.000 |
| shared-bias share of a member's squared error | **0.068** | 0 |

**The errors are independent, and they are variance, not bias.** The committee's residual shrinks
at almost exactly the 1/√M rate. Averaging 16 models removes **93 %** of a member's squared 1-step
error. Ensembling is nowhere near a ceiling — theory says M = 12 should still be cutting error
variance by 12×.

**And planning does not follow.** The same committees that nearly annihilate 1-step latent error
land at 0.57–0.61 and never beat their own best member. So:

> **Reducing 1-step latent prediction error — by any means, including the near-perfect reduction
> an ensemble demonstrably achieves — does not buy planning success.**

That is the same conclusion four other instruments reached, now from a fifth and much more direct
angle: M3's oracle ladder (learned objective + **oracle dynamics** = 0.913 against 0.427 for
learned dynamics, `2026-09-03` §16.1); the objective screen, where every in-isolation error metric
turned out to be a collapse detector (§2); `Spearman(ρ, CEM) = +0.028` from `spectral.py` (§5.1);
and six rounds of objective interventions that are uniformly null or negative.

**The 5-step follow-up was run, and it closes the caveat.** Independence at one step does not
imply independence once errors compound through a shared dynamics map, so the same probe was run
at **K = 5 — the CEM planning horizon** — chaining through the model's own `_chain_rollout`, the
object R2's consistency loss and T6's overshoot control both use, so it is not a
re-implementation that could manufacture its own answer.

| | K = 1 | **K = 5 (the planner's horizon)** |
|---|---|---|
| ρ̄ | +0.0010 | **−0.0008** |
| range | [−0.015, +0.016] | [−0.035, +0.041] |
| shrink observed / 1/√M | 1.040 | **1.000** |
| shared-bias share | 0.068 | 0.063 |

**Compounding creates no correlation.** At the horizon the planner actually uses, the committee's
error shrinks at *exactly* the independence rate — ratio 1.000. So the finding is not an artefact
of measuring one step: across the whole rollout, these models fail in unrelated directions, and
averaging them removes the failure at the theoretical maximum rate while planning stays flat.

The remaining caveat is honest and small: a **single fixed batch of 64** windows, and residuals
measured in the encoder's latent space rather than in the environment. Both are the same
conditions under which every other diagnostic in this campaign is read.

**What this leaves as the bottleneck.** Not the 1-step objective, which is now excluded about as
firmly as this campaign can exclude anything. Not compounding either, on current evidence: T6's
`jump5` vs `overshoot5` is −0.010 at n = 6, and R1 sweeps K to settle it. What survives is the
possibility M4 already put a number on — **the planner exploits its own model's errors**: CEM's
predicted improvement beats the realised one on **38 %** of baseline episodes but only **6.7 %**
under oracle dynamics. Independent errors are exactly what a maximiser over 300 candidates can
find and exploit, and averaging them does not remove the *exploitable* direction, only its
magnitude. That is a testable story, and it is where round 6's two healthy families already
point — R3 SAM (adversarial robustness in action space, the one arm whose action sensitivity does
not degrade) and R2 (consistency on the planner's own query distribution).

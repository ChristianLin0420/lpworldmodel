Why PiWM is not beating LpWM

Root-cause analysis on measured codes and measured CEM success. Generated 2026-09-01 14:04 · no new variants proposed.

Root cause (Step 3, support gating). The gate binarises its input — src = (x>0) if gate_input=="support" else x — so the support is a deterministic function of the code. By the data-processing inequality I(s;Y) ≤ I(z;Y) for every target: support gating is bounded above by magnitude gating and cannot win on information. Measured on trained codes, the support retains only 63% of the code's one-step predictive information — so the gate is handed 37% less to work with, in exchange for an inductive bias that has nothing to regularise at 2 epochs on a 4-dimensional task.
Root cause (Step 4, union head). The SDR union property holds only while the union stays sparse. At the operating density ρ = 0.55, OR-ing 4 readouts turns on 96.0% of the code — saturated, carrying almost nothing. Gradient descent escapes by crushing density, and both union arms converge to ≈1–10 active units of 384 with effective dimension below the task's own (4.31). The mechanism engages correctly (head_gap ≈ 0.32–0.40); the representation collapses underneath it.
What the theory got right. The support genuinely does carry most of the structure — 63% of the predictive information, from 24% of the bits. That is a real and non-obvious validation of the TBT premise. The error was applying an SDR operation to a representation that is not an SDR: in a true SDR magnitudes do not exist, so discarding them costs nothing. Here they exist and carry the other 37%.
